My View on AI-Assisted Engineering

Many of the tools and projects presented on this site did not begin with the current generation of Artificial Intelligence. Some of them were designed, prototyped, or developed by me long before AI-assisted development became widely available.

What has changed is the scale at which I can now revisit, improve, extend, and experiment with those ideas as a single engineer.

I see Artificial Intelligence as a multiplier for engineering experience, not as a replacement for it.

My experience as a software engineer gives me the foundation to define architectures, understand constraints, evaluate technical decisions, recognize incorrect assumptions, debug problems, and determine whether a proposed solution actually makes sense. AI can accelerate exploration and implementation, but engineering judgment remains essential.

For me, the most interesting part of AI-assisted development is not simply generating more code. It is being able to explore more alternatives, revisit older projects, automate repetitive work, investigate unfamiliar areas faster, build prototypes, improve documentation, analyze problems, and turn ideas into useful software at a scale that would previously have been much more difficult for one person.

Some of the projects published here are examples of that process: ideas and tools that already existed in some form, but that I have been able to substantially improve, extend, or complete with AI as part of my engineering workflow.

I do not view AI as an opponent, nor as something that diminishes the value of software engineering. I prefer to work with it much as I would work alongside a highly experienced technical colleague: discussing ideas, questioning suggestions, comparing alternatives, learning from unfamiliar approaches, correcting mistakes when necessary, and solving difficult problems together.

This is not about blindly accepting generated solutions. My approach is an engineering loop: design, question, verify, test, refine, and learn.

I believe that the combination of real software engineering experience, technical curiosity, and effective collaboration with AI can make an individual engineer considerably more capable. AI gives me a way to amplify what I already know while continuously learning new things, rather than replacing the knowledge and judgment I have built throughout my career.

That is one of the things I want this site to demonstrate: what an experienced software engineer can build when AI is treated as a technical partner and force multiplier, and how that combination can translate into useful software and a meaningful contribution to an engineering team.

Engineering Responsibility

AI can accelerate exploration, implementation, documentation, and learning, but I remain responsible for the engineering decisions behind the result.

I expect AI-assisted work to be questioned, verified, tested, debugged, and refined just like any other engineering work.
